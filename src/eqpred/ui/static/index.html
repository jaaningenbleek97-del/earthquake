What these numbers mean

A card is a chance that a first large earthquake happens in that place, in that window. First means we do not count aftershocks of a quake that already counted.

How it works

Each morning the lab writes a chance that a first large earthquake will happen. This week shows the latest working cards. Saved calls keeps every card already written and scores it when the window closes. This is research, not a warning.

From a measurement to a score

  1. 1

    Readings arrive with a time

    Solar wind, GPS, tides, the earthquake catalog, and the other feeds each carry the time we could have known them. A morning is only allowed to use what was already in hand.

  2. 2

    Start from the usual rate

    Before any signal, the chance is how often that quake followed a morning in the last five years. Only windows that have already finished can move that rate, so a quake today cannot raise today’s card.

  3. 3

    Signals nudge the 3-day card

    The 3-day card starts from the same kind of model as the 7-day and 30-day cards. Readings then add on top. Copies of one measurement share one ceiling, and those extra points stay between half and 2.5× the usual rate.

  4. 4

    The 30-day card stays locked

    The main screen shows the latest working cards. Every call already written stays on Saved calls, including the 21 September 2026 exam. A later fit writes a new call for a new morning. It does not rewrite a call that is already on file. Years from 2019 on are the exam. Those 30-day windows close around 21 October 2026.

  5. 5

    We score only after the window closes

    A hit is a first large quake in that place, inside the window, at or above that card’s magnitude. We compare the card with the usual rate. A card that sits high every day is not a success just because a quake eventually happens.

The question

Will a first large earthquake happen in this place, in this window?

First means the quake is large enough, and it is not an aftershock of one we already counted. A later shock in the same box, no larger and within about 200 km and 10 days of that quake, does not count again.

The four places

Earth-wide

Magnitude 7 or larger, anywhere on Earth. The 3-day card uses the same cutoff.

Hikurangi

Magnitude 5 or larger on the locked 30-day card. The 3-day card counts from magnitude 4.5, which is as low as this catalog goes.

Alpine Fault

Magnitude 5 or larger on the locked card. The 3-day card counts from magnitude 4.5.

Kermadec

Magnitude 6 or larger on the locked card. The 3-day card counts from magnitude 5.

Japan and Chile are practice boxes. They test the method. They do not get a live card, and they are not drawn on the globe.

How to read a number

26 in 100

In 100 mornings like this, we would expect the quake about 26 times.

2.5× the usual rate

The extra readings on the 3-day card can add up to one and a half times the usual rate on top of the shared model, or take away as much as half.

0.5× the usual rate

The most those extra readings can subtract is half the usual rate. The shared model underneath can still sit lower or higher than that.

Which card is which

Locked 30-day card

The official call. We score this one. We do not change it after we see whether the quake happened.

Working 30-day model

The same question, still allowed to change. It sits beside the locked card and does not replace it.

3-day card

The same kind of model as the longer cards, for the next 3 days, plus the reading votes on top. It does not replace the locked card.

7-day card

The locked recipe, asking about the next 7 days. It does not replace the 30-day card.

Research cards

Extra questions, such as which part of a fault, or whether slow slip is on. They do not replace the locked card.
