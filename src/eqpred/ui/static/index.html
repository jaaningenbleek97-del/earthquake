What these numbers mean

Each card is a 30-day chance of a first large quake — not aftershocks of one that already happened. The published number uses every dated sensor we can replay, sitting on that morning’s typical five-year rate. We only score windows that have already finished, so a future quake cannot move today’s number. Japan and Chile test the method; they never get a live card. A chance of 24 in 100 means: in 100 similar months we would expect about 24 hits. This is a research product, not a public warning.

How a published chance is made

Each row is a dated call: a 30-day window, a place, a magnitude floor, and either a chance or an alert. Earth-wide calls ask about magnitude 7+. Hikurangi and the Alpine Fault ask about magnitude 5+. Kermadec asks about magnitude 6+. Hits are judged on the final earthquake bulletin, not the first automatic location.
